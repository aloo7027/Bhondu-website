01

Log-Trig Essentials

Most common pivot points in advanced calculus
Log-Sine
\[\int_0^{\pi/2} \ln(\sin x)\,dx = -\frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2\]
Proved by splitting \(\sin x = 2\sin(x/2)\cos(x/2)\) and applying self-similarity of the integral.
Log-Cosine
\[\int_0^{\pi/2} \ln(\cos x)\,dx = -\frac{\pi}{2}\ln 2\]
Equals the log-sine integral by the substitution \(x \mapsto \pi/2 - x\).
Log-Tangent
\[\int_0^{\pi/2} \ln(\tan x)\,dx = 0\]
Follows from \(\ln\tan x = \ln\sin x - \ln\cos x\) and the two results above cancelling.
Inverse Trig Log — Catalan's Constant
\[\int_0^1 \frac{\arctan x}{x}\,dx = G \approx 0.9159\ldots\]
Catalan's constant \(G = \sum_{n=0}^\infty \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)^2}\). Expand \(\arctan x\) as a power series to see it directly.
Log-Sine Power
\[\int_0^{\pi} x\ln(\sin x)\,dx = -\frac{\pi^2}{2}\ln 2\]
Uses the Fourier series \(\ln\sin x = -\ln 2 - \sum_{k=1}^\infty \frac{\cos 2kx}{k}\) and integrates term by term.
02

Rational & Irrational Beasts

Require King's property, algebraic hacks, or contour methods
Gaussian Integral
\[\int_0^{\infty} e^{-x^2}\,dx = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\]
The polar-coordinates squaring trick. The full real-line version is \(\sqrt{\pi}\). Foundation of probability theory.
Dirichlet / Sinc Integral
\[\int_0^{\infty} \frac{\sin x}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}\]
Proved via Feynman's trick with \(I(a) = \int_0^\infty \frac{\sin x}{x}e^{-ax}dx\), or by residues. Conditionally convergent, not absolutely.
Fresnel Integral
\[\int_0^{\infty} \sin(x^2)\,dx = \sqrt{\frac{\pi}{8}}\]
Same value for \(\cos(x^2)\). Proved using contour integration over a sector of angle \(\pi/4\).
Log-Rational Zero
\[\int_0^{\infty} \frac{\ln x}{1+x^2}\,dx = 0\]
The substitution \(x \mapsto 1/x\) sends the integral to its own negative. A clean symmetry argument.
Quartic Identity
\[\int_0^{\infty} \frac{dx}{1+x^4} = \frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{2}}\]
Factor \(1+x^4\) over \(\mathbb{R}\) into two quadratics, then partial fractions. Also follows from the Beta function.
Log-Plus-One
\[\int_0^1 \frac{\ln(1+x)}{1+x^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{8}\ln 2\]
Feynman's trick with parameter inside \(\ln(1+ax)\). A beautiful two-page derivation.
Ahmed's Integral — "Legendary"
\[\int_0^1 \frac{\arctan\!\left(\sqrt{x^2+2}\right)}{(x^2+2)\sqrt{x^2+1}}\,dx = \frac{5\pi^2}{96}\]
Discovered by Zafar Ahmed (2002). Requires a double-parameter Feynman attack. A staple of integration competitions.
Sinc-Squared
\[\int_0^{\infty} \frac{\sin^2 x}{x^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}\]
Integrate by parts once to reduce to the Dirichlet sinc integral, or use Parseval's theorem on \(\text{rect}(\xi)\).
03

Infinite Series & Zeta Connections

Where integration meets number theory
Basel Problem Integral
\[\int_0^1 \frac{-\ln(1-x)}{x}\,dx = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}\]
Expand \(-\ln(1-x) = \sum x^n/n\) and integrate term by term. Direct meeting point of analysis and number theory.
Zeta(3) Gateway — General Form
Zeta
\[\int_0^{\infty} \frac{x^{n-1}}{e^x - 1}\,dx = \Gamma(n)\,\zeta(n)\]
At \(n=3\): gives \(\Gamma(3)\zeta(3) = 2\zeta(3)\) (Apéry's constant \(\approx 1.202\)). Note: the original list wrote this incorrectly — the general form is essential.
Polylog Base
\[\int_0^1 \frac{\ln x}{1-x}\,dx = -\frac{\pi^2}{6}\]
Expand \(1/(1-x)\) as a geometric series. This is \(-\text{Li}_2(1) = -\zeta(2)\).
Alternating Series Integral
\[\int_0^1 \frac{\ln x}{1+x}\,dx = -\frac{\pi^2}{12}\]
Expand using the alternating geometric series. Equals \(-\frac{1}{2}\zeta(2)\). Relates to \(\eta(2)\) (Dirichlet eta function).
Gamma Function — The Full Definition
Gamma
\[\int_0^{\infty} x^{s-1} e^{-x}\,dx = \Gamma(s), \quad s > 0\]
At positive integers: \(\Gamma(n) = (n-1)!\). This is the master result. Note: the original list labelled this "Exponential Integral" — that name belongs to \(\text{Ei}(x)\), a different function entirely.
04

Symmetry & Parameter Hacks

Integrals that collapse with the right trick
Master Symmetry — King's Property
Symmetry
\[\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{\sin^n x}{\sin^n x + \cos^n x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{4}\]
Works for any \(n \in \mathbb{R}\). The substitution \(x \mapsto \pi/2 - x\) sends the integrand to its complement; adding gives 1.
Log-Trig Scaling
\[\int_0^{\infty} \frac{\ln(1+x^2)}{1+x^2}\,dx = \pi\ln 2\]
Feynman's trick with \(I(a) = \int_0^\infty \frac{\ln(1+a^2x^2)}{1+x^2}dx\), differentiate w.r.t. \(a\).
Frullani's Integral
Feynman
\[\int_0^{\infty} \frac{f(ax) - f(bx)}{x}\,dx = \bigl(f(0) - f(\infty)\bigr)\ln\!\frac{a}{b}\]
Requires \(f\) continuous with finite limits at 0 and \(\infty\). Classic: \(f = e^{-x}\) gives \(\int_0^\infty \frac{e^{-ax}-e^{-bx}}{x}dx = \ln(b/a)\).
Cauchy / Laplace Identity
Contour
\[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \frac{\cos x}{a^2 + x^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{a}e^{-a}, \quad a > 0\]
Close the contour in the upper half-plane; single pole at \(x = ia\). A model residue calculation.
Sine Integral Product
\[\int_0^{\infty} \frac{\sin(ax)\cos(bx)}{x}\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}, \quad a > b > 0\]
Use the product-to-sum identity \(2\sin A\cos B = \sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)\), then apply Dirichlet's result to each piece.
05

High-Level Special Results

The "grand finale" tier
Digamma Relation
Beta/Gamma
\[\int_0^1 \frac{x^{a-1} - x^{b-1}}{1-x}\,dx = \psi(b) - \psi(a)\]
Where \(\psi = \Gamma'/\Gamma\) is the digamma function. Fundamental tool for evaluating log-polynomial integrals over \([0,1]\).
Beta Function — Euler's First Integral
Beta/Gamma
\[\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^m x\cos^n x\,dx = \frac{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{m+1}{2}\right)\Gamma\!\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}{2\,\Gamma\!\left(\frac{m+n+2}{2}\right)}\]
Derived via \(B(p,q) = \frac{\Gamma(p)\Gamma(q)}{\Gamma(p+q)}\) with \(p = (m+1)/2\), \(q = (n+1)/2\). Valid for \(m,n > -1\).
Reflection Formula
Gamma
\[\Gamma(s)\,\Gamma(1-s) = \frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}, \quad s \notin \mathbb{Z}\]
Euler's reflection formula. Unlocks dozens of integrals — whenever you see \(\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)\), this collapses it to a clean trig expression.
Log-Square Integral
\[\int_0^1 \frac{\ln^2 x}{1+x^2}\,dx = \frac{\pi^3}{16}\]
Expand \(1/(1+x^2)\) as an alternating series, integrate \(x^{2k}\ln^2 x\) using \(\int_0^1 x^n \ln^2 x\,dx = \frac{2}{(n+1)^3}\), and recognise \(\beta(3)\).
Inverse Log — Euler-Mascheroni
\[\int_0^1 \ln\!\left(\ln\frac{1}{x}\right)dx = -\gamma \approx -0.5772\ldots\]
Follows from differentiating \(\Gamma(s) = \int_0^\infty x^{s-1}e^{-x}dx\) at \(s=1\). A deep connection between calculus and the Euler–Mascheroni constant.
Grand Finale
\[\int_0^{\infty} \frac{\ln(1+x^4)}{1+x^2}\,dx = \pi\ln(1+\sqrt{2})\]
Requires Feynman's trick with the parameter inside \(\ln(1+ax^4)\), combined with partial fractions over \(\mathbb{C}\). A true showpiece result.
Power-Log Radical
Beta/Gamma
\[\int_0^1 \frac{dx}{\sqrt[n]{1-x^n}} = \frac{\sqrt{\pi}\;\Gamma(1/n)}{n\;\Gamma\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n}+\tfrac{1}{2}\right)}\]
Substitute \(u = x^n\), then recognise the Beta integral \(B\!\left(\tfrac{1}{n}, \tfrac{1}{2}\right)\) and apply the Gamma relation.

Feynman's Trick Templates

Differentiation under the integral sign — added to original list
Log-Ratio via Parameter
Feynman
\[\int_0^1 \frac{x^a - x^b}{\ln x}\,dx = \ln\frac{a+1}{b+1}\]
Define \(I(a) = \int_0^1 x^a/\ln x\,dx\), differentiate, solve the ODE, and integrate back. The logarithm in the integrand is "unlocked" by differentiation.
Exponential Sinc — Laplace Form
Feynman
\[\int_0^{\infty} \frac{\sin(bx)}{x}\,e^{-ax}\,dx = \arctan\!\frac{b}{a}, \quad a > 0\]
Differentiate w.r.t. \(b\) to get a simple rational integral. As \(a \to 0^+\), recovers Dirichlet's \(\pi/2\).
Gaussian with Linear Exponent
Feynman
\[\int_0^{\infty} e^{-ax^2}\,dx = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{a}}, \quad a > 0\]
Differentiate w.r.t. \(a\) to generate \(\int x^{2n} e^{-ax^2}dx\) for all orders — the entire Gaussian integral family from one result.
Log-Cosine Family
Feynman
\[\int_0^{\infty} e^{-ax}\cos(bx)\,dx = \frac{a}{a^2+b^2}, \quad a > 0\]
Paired with the sine version \(\frac{b}{a^2+b^2}\). Combine as the real and imaginary parts of \(\int_0^\infty e^{-(a-ib)x}dx\).

Polylogarithm & Zeta Ladder

Added to original list — essential for competition-level problems
Log² over (1+x)
Zeta
\[\int_0^1 \frac{\ln^2 x}{1+x}\,dx = \frac{7}{4}\,\zeta(3)\]
Expand \(1/(1+x)\) as alternating geometric series and integrate \(x^n \ln^2 x\) term by term. Connects to Apéry's constant.
Log × Log(1−x)
Zeta
\[\int_0^1 \ln x \cdot \ln(1-x)\,dx = 2 - \frac{\pi^2}{6}\]
Expand \(\ln(1-x) = -\sum x^n/n\), integrate term by term using \(\int_0^1 x^n \ln x\,dx = -1/(n+1)^2\).
Dilogarithm at 1
Polylog
\[\int_0^1 \frac{\text{Li}_2(x)}{x}\,dx = \zeta(3)\]
The polylogarithm ladder: \(\text{Li}_2(x) = -\int_0^x \frac{\ln(1-t)}{t}dt\). Iterate to build \(\text{Li}_3\), \(\text{Li}_4\), connecting to all \(\zeta(2k+1)\).
Log² over (1−x)
Zeta
\[\int_0^1 \frac{\ln^2 x}{1-x}\,dx = 2\,\zeta(3)\]
Same technique as the \(\ln^2/(1+x)\) case but with geometric (non-alternating) series. Gives twice Apéry's constant.
Clausen / Log-Sine² Integral
Zeta
\[\int_0^{\pi/2} x^2\,dx = \frac{\pi^3}{24}, \quad \int_0^{\pi/2} \ln^2(\sin x)\,dx = \frac{\pi}{2}\!\left(\ln^2 2 + \frac{\pi^2}{12}\right)\]
The second is a deep result proved via the Fourier series of \(\ln\sin x\). Clausen's function \(\text{Cl}_2\) generalises this family.
Parseval — Sinc² via Fourier
Parseval
\[\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left(\frac{\sin x}{x}\right)^2 dx = \pi\]
Apply Parseval's theorem to the rectangle function whose Fourier transform is \(\text{sinc}\). No contour integration needed — a slick one-liner.
08

Technique Map

Which tool opens which door

⟳ Symmetry / King's Property

If \(\int_a^b f(x)\,dx\) has a reflection symmetry under \(x \mapsto a+b-x\), add the two forms. Instantly collapses any integral of the form \(f/(f+g)\) where \(g(x)=f(a+b-x)\).

∂/∂a Feynman's Trick

Insert a parameter \(a\) into the integrand, differentiate under the integral sign, solve the simpler integral, then integrate back in \(a\). Best when there's a log or trig in the integrand with no natural series expansion.

∑ Series Expansion

Expand a factor (e.g. \(\ln(1-x)\), \(1/(1\pm x)\), \(e^x\)) as a power series, integrate term by term, then recognise the resulting series as \(\zeta(s)\), \(\eta(s)\), \(\beta(s)\), or \(G\).

B/Γ Beta & Gamma Machinery

Convert to the form \(\int_0^1 x^{p-1}(1-x)^{q-1}dx = B(p,q)\) or \(\int_0^\infty x^{s-1}e^{-x}dx = \Gamma(s)\). Euler's reflection formula \(\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s) = \pi/\sin(\pi s)\) then closes many results in closed form.

∮ Contour Integration

For rational or rational-times-trig integrands over \((-\infty, \infty)\) or \((0, \infty)\): close in the upper half-plane, apply the residue theorem. Keyhole contours handle branch cuts (\(x^s\) terms). Sector contours handle \(e^{ix^2}\) (Fresnel).

Li Polylogarithm Ladder

Recognise that \(\text{Li}_n(x) = \int_0^x \text{Li}_{n-1}(t)/t\,dt\) and \(\text{Li}_2(1)=\zeta(2)\), \(\text{Li}_3(1)=\zeta(3)\). Integrate by parts iteratively to climb the ladder and identify \(\zeta\) values at each rung.

≈ Glasser's Master Theorem

States that \(\int_{-\infty}^\infty f\!\left(x - \frac{1}{x}\right)dx = \int_{-\infty}^\infty f(x)\,dx\). The substitution \(x \mapsto x - 1/x\) leaves the integral invariant — it collapses integrands with this hidden structure.

♩ Parseval / Plancherel

If \(\hat{f}(\xi)\) is the Fourier transform of \(f\), then \(\int |f|^2 = \int |\hat{f}|^2\). Convert a hard integral to a product of simple Fourier transforms. Optimal for sinc-type integrals and convolutions.
